What is hiragana?

Hiragana is a syllabary used in the japanese language used to write text.

In addition to hiragana, Japanese also uses katakana, a syllabary for proper nouns, and kanji, which are ideograms that represent entire words or concepts.

Hiragana is usually employed to write short vocabs that are grammatically relevant (such as particles, 助動詞) and fragments of vocabs that can change based on the state of the main vocab, while kanji are used for the immutable parts of vocabs.

Japanese also use romanji, which consists on the western alphabet, in order to represent japanese text in a legible format for foreign readers. Romanji also helps japanese readers to read foreign text.
